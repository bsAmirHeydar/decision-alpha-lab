# F2 Zone

F2 can create reversal potential, but it is usually less execution-stable than Hook or F1 because its parent-timeframe stop edge is often broad, unstable, or unclear.

The system must not treat F2 as a direct pattern. It must translate F2 into movement limitation and then ask whether that limitation creates a measurable zone.

## Main F2 Families

### F2 Without Waist Hit

F2 can form a 1/2 relationship without hitting its waist. This can create reversal potential, but the parent stop is usually not stable. It is generally a watch zone that requires lower-timeframe refinement.

### F2 Waist-Hit Point 1/2 Case

In the corrected doctrine, when F2 hits its own waist, that waist-hit can become Point 1. The next relevant hit/extension becomes Point 2. From Point 2 onward/beyond, reversal potential becomes active.

```text
F2 waist-hit = Point 1
following hit/extension = Point 2
from Point 2 onward/beyond = active reversal potential field
```

This is still not automatically a direct parent-timeframe trade. It normally requires a lower-timeframe child zone with a clear start and stop.

### F2 Symmetry with F1

When F2 exceeds the symmetry of F1, a broad reversal-potential area can appear. This area is not safe by itself because it does not naturally provide a stable stop. It requires child-zone refinement.

## Policy

```text
F2 can tell us where reversal potential exists.
F2 does not automatically tell us where to place a safe stop.
No stable stop = no direct F2 trade.
```

## Execution Role

F2 is primarily a parent-context source. It becomes tradable only when a child structure creates a valid [[Zone_as_Risk_Contract]].
