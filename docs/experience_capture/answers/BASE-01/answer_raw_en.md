# BASE-01 — Raw Answer

## Topic

Multi-state market interpretation: direction and context.

## Original Experience, translated to English

The market's multi-state nature means that two categories must always be considered: direction and context.

Direction means bullish or bearish.

Context means Hook or Rally.

So, even in a bullish view, we must ask:

- If we assume we are in a Rally, how should we read it and count it?
- If we assume we are in a Hook, how should we read it, where are we inside it, and how should it be counted?

The same must be done for the bearish side.

This multi-state view must also be read fractally. That means we must look at these multiple states across different timeframes.

In Image 1, even though the market has had a bearish Rally, that Rally itself is inside a Hook where the cycle can be seen. But the Hook has not yet been hit/completed. Where we are inside the Hook is important, because the counting tells us whether the Hook is closing now or whether it is still open and has more continuation.

In Image 2, if we look from a bullish perspective, the first Hook has closed and now the market is making a Rally. Inside the Rally, we have F-counting until F3 forms and the trend finishes. But at the same time, this Rally itself is inside a larger Hook whose extreme has not even formed yet. The market may even reverse from the current live location, and then we may realize that what we thought was a Rally was actually building a Hook.

Because the market can reshape itself into different forms at any time, it must be read in multiple states. The same logic must then be applied across different timeframes.
