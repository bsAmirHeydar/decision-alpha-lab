# OBS0001 — Apparent Importance of Structural Highs and Lows

## Observation

Throughout the development, testing, and manual execution of multiple trading approaches, a recurring pattern was observed:

Strategies that ignored prior structural highs and lows generally exhibited poor practical performance.

Conversely, strategies that explicitly incorporated these structures often produced better trading outcomes.

However, these improvements were not sufficiently stable, objective, or systematic to justify full automation.

## Context

This observation emerged from repeated interaction with the market rather than from formal statistical analysis.

It reflects accumulated practical experience rather than established evidence.

## What Was Observed

The following recurring phenomena were noted around prior structural highs and lows:

- Not all price locations appeared equally important.
- Historical highs and lows frequently coincided with meaningful changes in market behavior.
- Ignoring these structures often degraded strategy performance.
- Incorporating them appeared beneficial, although inconsistently.
- The speed of price movement often changed noticeably near these locations.
- Price sometimes appeared to become "stuck" or congested around these areas before resolving.
- Certain structural levels exhibited unusually forceful or abrupt breakouts.
- The persistence of movement beyond these levels appeared to vary substantially.
- Similar price movements occurring away from such structures often appeared behaviorally different.

## What Is NOT Claimed

This observation does not imply that:

- structural highs and lows possess predictive power,
- these locations are true decision nodes,
- the observed differences are statistically significant,
- the observed phenomena can generate persistent alpha,
- the mechanisms underlying these behaviors are understood.

## Motivation

The repeated appearance of these phenomena motivates formal hypothesis generation and quantitative investigation.

In particular, it motivates the question of whether structural highs and lows contain objectively measurable information regarding subsequent market behavior.
